Evidence-Seeking Multimodal Agent

What should a multimodal agent do when the available visual evidence is insufficient to make a reliable decision?

A standard VLM must answer from the evidence it receives. An evidence-seeking agent can instead estimate whether its current decision is sufficiently grounded, identify what evidence is missing, and interact with the environment before committing to an answer.

Sequential decision problem

\[ s_t = (o_{1:t},\, g_t,\, h_t,\, u_t) \]
\[ a_t \sim \pi_\theta(a \mid s_t), \qquad a_t \in \{\text{acquire},\text{retrieve},\text{ask},\text{predict},\text{abstain}\} \]
\[ o_{t+1} = \mathcal{E}(s_t,a_t), \qquad s_{t+1} = \operatorname{Update}(s_t,o_{t+1}) \]

\(g_t\) is the currently grounded visual evidence, \(h_t\) the current hypothesis, and \(u_t\) evidence insufficiency. The policy chooses whether more evidence is worth acquiring, not only the final answer.

Reward

Acquisition is not rewarded by itself; otherwise the model can keep requesting more evidence.

\[ R = R_{\mathrm{task}} +\lambda_g R_{\mathrm{ground}} +\lambda_v R_{\mathrm{verify}} +\lambda_i R_{\mathrm{info}} -\lambda_c C(a) -\lambda_r N_{\mathrm{redundant}} \]

\(R_{\mathrm{task}}\): correctness of the eventual decision. \(R_{\mathrm{ground}}\): dependence on task-relevant visual evidence. \(R_{\mathrm{verify}}\): counterfactual / controlled verification. \(R_{\mathrm{info}}\): whether the new observation improves the evidence state. \(C(a)\): cost of another observation. \(N_{\mathrm{redundant}}\): unnecessary or repeated acquisition.

Can a multimodal policy learn not only to reason over visual evidence, but also when that evidence is inadequate and which action would most efficiently resolve the ambiguity?

The trigger

The strongest part is not acquisition itself. Active perception already asks which observation would reduce uncertainty. The question here is different: when should the model decide that its current visual evidence does not justify its answer?

\[ I_t = f(G_t,\, V_t,\, C_t) \]
\[ a_t = \begin{cases} \text{decide}, & I_t < \tau \\[3pt] \text{acquire evidence}, & I_t \ge \tau \end{cases} \]

\(G_t\) measures grounding quality, \(V_t\) stability under evidence interventions, and \(C_t\) decision confidence. Evidence insufficiency is defined in terms of grounding, not only token entropy.

Progression

Clinical-description-guided grounding → Does the prediction depend on relevant evidence? → Controlled / counterfactual verification → Turn verification into a learnable reward → Multimodal RLVR → Evidence insufficient? → Learn an acquisition policy → Acquire → re-ground → verify → decide

The agentic loop is the next step after grounding and verification, not a separate “multimodal agents” project.
