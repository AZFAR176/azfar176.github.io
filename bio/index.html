Why This Direction

Standard multimodal evaluation often measures final answers without explicitly validating how evidence was acquired, grounded, and used. Our work targets this gap through verifiable multimodal reasoning pipelines where model behavior can be tested, stressed, and improved with feedback signals that are measurable.

Selected Research Thread

Multimodal Oral Cancer Screening

Tanuh.ai, MoE AI Centre of Excellence, IISc

Developed smartphone-based models across diverse acquisition settings and populations, with a focus on reliable grounding, robustness to capture variation, and intervention-aware verification signals.

Grounding and Alignment Link

Connected representation-level diagnostics, controlled interventions, and post-training objectives so multimodal systems can improve beyond pure scaling.

Grounded Multimodal Reasoning Flow

1. Problem Reliable reasoning from smartphone clinical images under practical, noisy real-world conditions.
2. Grounding Align clinical language with relevant visual regions so decisions depend on evidence, not priors.
3. Robustness Stress-test behavior under acquisition variance, viewpoint shifts, and nuisance factors.
4. Evidence Verification Use controlled and counterfactual interventions to test whether predicted reasoning is causally supported.
5. Post-Training Convert grounding and counterfactual feedback into verifiable reward signals for RLVR-style optimization.
6. Future Direction: Evidence-Seeking Multimodal Agents When evidence is insufficient, agents should actively acquire missing views/context, re-ground, and then decide with calibrated uncertainty.

What This Enables

  • A coherent research arc from perception and grounding to verifiable post-training.
  • Model improvements driven by measurable behavior rather than only final-answer metrics.
  • A practical path toward safer multimodal agents operating under uncertainty and shift.
