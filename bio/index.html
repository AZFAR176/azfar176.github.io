I work on multimodal and language models, with recent research on visual grounding, verifiable feedback, RL-based post-training, and agentic systems.

A central question in my work is how to verify that model predictions are supported by relevant visual evidence, and how such feedback can be used to improve reasoning through post-training or additional evidence acquisition.

Grounded Multimodal Reasoning

My work at Tanuh.ai / the Ministry of Education AI Centre of Excellence at IISc studied smartphone-based oral cancer screening across diverse Indian populations and acquisition settings.

Beyond predictive performance, I have been interested in whether multimodal models base their decisions on clinically relevant visual cues, how this behaviour changes under controlled interventions, and whether these signals can provide verifiable feedback for post-training.

Research Flow

1. Clinical Visual Grounding Clinical descriptions guide the model toward task-relevant lesion regions, providing an explicit connection between clinical semantics and visual evidence.
↓
2. Robustness under Acquisition Shift Evaluate whether predictions and grounding remain stable across changes in patients, devices, image quality, viewpoint, and acquisition conditions.
↓
3. Controlled & Counterfactual Verification Use controlled interventions to test invariance to nuisance changes, and counterfactual interventions to test whether predictions respond when task-relevant visual cues are altered.
↓
4. Verifiable Multimodal Post-Training Use grounding and counterfactual signals as verifiable feedback for multimodal RLVR, moving beyond outcome-only supervision toward evidence-aware rewards.
↓
5. Active Evidence Acquisition Extend verification toward agents that recognize when current evidence is insufficient and acquire an informative new observation before deciding.

Future Direction: Evidence-Seeking Multimodal Agents

A grounded model can still receive an image that is ambiguous, incomplete, or too low-quality to support a reliable decision. Rather than forcing a prediction, I am interested in multimodal agents that can recognize missing evidence and select an informative next action.

Possible actions include requesting another view, examining a region more closely, asking for relevant clinical context, retrieving supporting information, or abstaining when sufficient evidence cannot be obtained.

Can a multimodal agent learn when its current evidence is insufficient, what additional evidence would be informative, and how to acquire it before making a decision?
